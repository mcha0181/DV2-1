01 / People

The progress paradox

National scores improved, but the remaining divide between different population groups is much larger than the gap between states. Compare change over two observed survey years, then investigate social differences.

Older Australians made faster gains than the national average, but that does not mean inequality disappeared. The gap depends on which groups—and which measure—we compare.

02 / Access and participation

A connection is not the same as capability

Access, affordability and ability are different dimensions of digital inclusion. The Census measure adds context about participation online but is not itself a digital skills test.

Important distinctionDigital inclusion scores come from the Australian Digital Inclusion Index. Online Census returns are an administrative participation measure, not an individual test of digital literacy. The 2025 affordability method changed, so avoid direct comparison with older affordability scores.

04 / Conclusion

Digital progress has not eliminated exclusion

Digital exclusion remains concentrated among older Australians and those without completed secondary school. These are associations in published group data, not evidence of cause and effect.

Nearly two in three older Australians face digital exclusion.
